# MTDRB / إدارة Full Marketing Playbook

**Prepared by Manus AI**

## Executive summary

MTDRB should be positioned as the **bilingual operating system for serious gyms and fitness studios in the Gulf and wider MENAT region**. Its strongest differentiator is not a single feature. It is the combination of one calm operating workspace with regional readiness: members, classes, trainers, attendance, billing, analytics, tasks, branches, English/Arabic localization, and RTL support.

The launch platform is:

> **Run the gym. Not the chaos.**

The first acquisition motion should be **trial- and demo-led**, supported by founder-led outreach and partnerships. The product is ready to show and measure, but the team should avoid aggressive paid scaling until attribution, activation, customer proof, production hosting, and the payment-provider decision are resolved.

The market context supports a business-operations narrative. The Health & Fitness Association reports that global fitness memberships increased 6% year over year in 2024, average revenue increased 8%, and 91% of surveyed operators expected revenue gains in 2025.[1] IMARC describes Saudi Arabia’s fitness-club market as expanding under Vision 2030, with digital transformation, women-focused facilities, hybrid models, and technology adoption shaping the sector.[2] These are directional market signals, not MTDRB performance claims.

## The customer and the problem

The primary customer is an owner, general manager, or operations lead at a gym, boutique studio, personal-training facility, or small-to-mid-sized fitness chain in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, or nearby MENAT markets. The best early customer is large enough to feel coordination pain but small enough to make a software decision quickly.

The customer’s daily problem is fragmentation. Members live in one place, classes in another, invoices in a spreadsheet, attendance in a check-in system, and follow-ups in a chat thread or someone’s memory. The cost is not only administrative time. It is slow decisions, missed renewals, double-bookings, inconsistent reporting, and a team that cannot answer basic operating questions without searching.

| Segment | Trigger to buy | Lead message | Best product proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-location gym | The owner is still managing the business from spreadsheets and chats. | “One dashboard for the whole gym.” | Dashboard, members, classes |
| Boutique studio | Class schedules, capacity, waitlists, and attendance are difficult to coordinate. | “More sessions. Fewer scheduling surprises.” | Calendar and attendance |
| Personal-training facility | Trainer workload and member follow-up are hard to see. | “Know who needs attention before they disappear.” | Member risk and trainer views |
| Multi-branch operator | Leadership lacks a reliable cross-branch view. | “Every branch. One operating rhythm.” | Branches, billing, analytics |
| Arabic-first or bilingual team | English-only tools create adoption and workflow friction. | “Built local. Not translated.” | Arabic UI and RTL layout |

## Positioning

**For GCC and MENAT gym operators who have outgrown fragmented admin, MTDRB is the bilingual gym operating system that unifies daily operations and makes growth visible. Unlike generic scheduling tools or spreadsheet workflows, MTDRB connects members, classes, trainers, billing, attendance, analytics, and branches in one region-ready workspace.**

The messaging architecture has three proof pillars:

| Pillar | Problem | Promise | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational clarity | Information is scattered across tools and people. | One live view of the business. | “Your whole gym. One glance.” |
| Retention and growth visibility | Owners notice churn too late. | Surface inactivity, renewal risk, and performance signals earlier. | “More renewals. Fewer surprises.” |
| Regional readiness | Global tools feel translated or incomplete. | English/Arabic, RTL, branch-aware, region-oriented workflows. | “Built local. Not translated.” |

The voice should be direct, confident, calm, and slightly anti-chaos. Use operator language such as “missed follow-ups,” “double-bookings,” “last invoice,” and “members who have gone quiet.” Avoid generic SaaS clichés, unverified ROI claims, fake urgency, and broad “AI-powered” promises without a specific demonstrable workflow.

## Offer and funnel

The repository currently implements a **14-day free trial with no credit card required**. The subscription flow creates a trial entitlement in Supabase, but it does not yet connect to a live payment processor or server-side payment webhook. Therefore, public messaging should market the trial honestly and should not imply that paid checkout is already live.

| Stage | User question | Asset | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | “Is this for a business like mine?” | Operator-led video, bilingual carousel, search content | Qualified reach |
| Consideration | “Will it solve my daily problems?” | Landing page, dashboard tour, feature pages | CTA rate |
| Conversion | “Can I try it safely?” | Signup flow, transparent plans, trial | Signup completion |
| Activation | “Can I get value quickly?” | Five-minute setup checklist and onboarding | Activated trial |
| Expansion | “Can I use it across my operation?” | Branch, analytics, billing, team workflows | Conversion intent |

Define an **activated trial** as a new workspace that creates or imports at least one member, creates a class, and views an operational or financial dashboard within seven days. This is more meaningful than raw signups and should be the main early growth metric.

## Channel strategy

Direct outreach and partnerships should lead the first 90 days because regional trust and workflow relevance matter more than broad reach. LinkedIn should reach owners and operators with practical insights. Instagram and TikTok should show the product visually in short, captioned walkthroughs. SEO should compound after the team hears the language real operators use in demos.

| Priority | Channel | First execution |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Direct outreach | Build a list of 100 target operators and invite them to a 15-minute workflow review. |
| 2 | Partnerships | Approach gym consultants, equipment distributors, trainers, payment providers, and associations for co-hosted operator sessions. |
| 3 | LinkedIn | Publish operator content on churn signals, class utilization, branch reporting, and spreadsheet replacement. |
| 4 | Instagram/TikTok | Publish 20–40 second screen-recorded workflows with Arabic/English captions. |
| 5 | Google Search | Build pages for gym management software, Arabic gym software, class scheduling, member retention, and GCC billing. |
| 6 | Email | Run a short welcome, activation, workflow, team-adoption, and conversion sequence. |

## 30-day launch rhythm

| Days | Focus | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 | Establish the category | Launch the “Run the gym. Not the chaos.” message, show the dashboard, poll operators about their biggest admin bottleneck, and publish the first founder note. |
| 8–14 | Demonstrate the workflows | Show scheduling, retention signals, billing visibility, and bilingual/RTL support. Publish the five-minute gym operations audit. |
| 15–21 | Build trust | Share an anonymized workflow story, explain role-based team access, publish transparent plan comparison, and start partner conversations. |
| 22–30 | Convert and learn | Retarget only after event tracking is reliable, publish the first SEO guide, run a recap campaign, and measure activated trials by source and message angle. |

## Measurement system

The repository now captures first-touch UTM parameters in session storage and emits placement-aware trial CTA events into a vendor-neutral `window.dataLayer`. The main events are `mtdrb_marketing_attribution_captured` and `mtdrb_trial_cta_click`, with CTA placements such as `hero`, `nav`, `mobile-nav`, `starter-pricing`, `pro-pricing`, and `final-cta`.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Qualified visitor | Visitor from a target market who views a product or pricing page. |
| CTA rate | Visitor who clicks Start Free Trial or a demo action. |
| Signup completion | User who completes the account-creation flow. |
| Activated trial | Workspace meeting the member, class, and dashboard activation definition within seven days. |
| Conversion intent | Trial user who selects a plan, requests payment setup, or books a conversion call. |
| Channel efficiency | Activated trials divided by channel cost. |

Do not scale paid acquisition until these events are connected to a reporting destination and validated against real signup and activation records.

## Claims policy

MTDRB can confidently market implemented capabilities visible in the repository: member management, class scheduling, trainer management, attendance, billing, analytics, tasks, branch management, English/Arabic localization, RTL support, role-based access, and tenant isolation.

Claims requiring qualification or proof before public use include “thousands of gyms,” verified retention or revenue improvement, customer logos, customer testimonials, “enterprise-grade” security, completed VAT compliance in every target country, named local payment-gateway processing, and any metrics currently represented only by demo data. The rule is simple: **show what exists, label what is illustrative, and verify what is claimed as an outcome.**
