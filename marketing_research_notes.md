# Marketing Research Notes

## Health & Fitness Association

Source: https://www.healthandfitness.org/2025-global-fitness-industry-report-shows-record-growth-and-whats-next-for-the-market/

The Health & Fitness Association reports that, globally, memberships rose 6% year over year in 2024, average revenue increased 8%, and facility counts expanded nearly 4%. It also reports that 91% of operators expected revenue gains in 2025, while 83% expected profitability gains. The article frames fitness as a resilient, expanding operating business rather than discretionary hobby spending. This supports positioning MTDRB around business visibility, operational control, retention, and profitable growth rather than generic fitness software.

Use with caution: the source summarizes a global survey, not GCC-specific performance. Do not present these numbers as MTDRB results or as direct market-size estimates for the Gulf.

## IMARC Saudi Arabia health and fitness club market

Source: https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-health-fitness-club-market

IMARC states that the Saudi health and fitness club market reached USD 1,260.4 million in 2025 and forecasts USD 2,808.2 million by 2034, with a 9.31% CAGR for 2026–2034. The page attributes growth to Vision 2030, investment, women-focused facilities, digital transformation, hybrid models, wearable adoption, and AI-enabled experiences. It also describes growth in women-focused facilities and technology integration.

Use with caution: this is a commercial market-research estimate. Cite it as directional context only, and avoid turning it into a precise TAM claim unless the methodology and scope are independently validated.

## Strategic implications for MTDRB

The initial wedge should be GCC/MENAT gym and boutique-studio operators who need a bilingual, region-aware operating system and who are moving from spreadsheets, chat threads, or fragmented tools toward centralization.

Saudi Arabia should be the first paid-acquisition test market because the repository already emphasizes MENAT localization, Arabic/RTL support, VAT workflows, multi-branch support, and regional payment language, while external market context indicates strong fitness-sector momentum and digital transformation.

The first campaign should sell operational calm and business visibility: one source of truth for members, classes, trainers, billing, attendance, and reporting. A secondary growth narrative should focus on catching inactivity and renewal risk early, but public claims should remain framed as product capabilities until verified customer outcome data exists.

A third differentiator is localization depth. The product should claim bilingual English/Arabic and RTL support only as implemented capabilities. Claims about local payment gateways and VAT compliance should be qualified until live integrations, legal requirements, and market-by-market coverage are verified.
